What makes an efficient LED?

1. Electrons and holes preferentially recombine emitting light
		High quality material
		Direct rather than indirect energy gaps
		
2.  Electrons and hole are close together

Electrons have momentum, could be at rest or moving one way or the other
$$
E_{ke} = \frac{1}{2}mv^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}
$$
GaAs is an example of a direct band-gap semiconductor.
Direct means that the electrons low momentum state matches the holes low momentum state, therefore the emission of a photon is fast and efficient. Only some semiconductor materials are good at conducting light, these are the ones with direct band gaps.

Compound semiconductors more likely to be a direct gap. You can combine compound materials to finely configure band gaps for your desired purpose.
